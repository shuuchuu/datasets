# French Press 1914, Questions and Answers

Questions about `french-press-1914-passages` for evaluating a question answering
(RAG) system, real answers of such a system with annotations, for checking an LLM
judge against them, and generated questions for training one (RAFT, a fine-tuned
encoder), drawn away from the test passages.

## Files

- `questions.jsonl`: 20 questions written one by one from passages read in full, one per line: `id`, `question`,
  `answer` (a reference answer, `null` for the 2 unanswerable questions), `passage_ids`
  (every passage of `french-press-1914-passages` found to state the answer; empty when
  there is none). 18 have an answer the corpus states, each with the passages found to
  state it (one for 16 of them, 2 and 6 for questions 3 and 8), and 2 the corpus can't
  answer, which the system should decline.
- `synthetic-questions.jsonl`: 60 questions generated from the corpus, one per line,
  each with an expert's verdict (43 valid): `id` (101 to 160, apart from the 20 above),
  `question`, `answer` (the reference answer: the generated one, or the expert's
  rewording when it was right but badly put), `generated_answer` (the generator's own),
  `source_passage_id` (the passage the question was generated from), `passage_ids`
  (the passages that state the answer, the source first; the source alone for a
  rejected question), `usable` (the generator's own `exploitable` flag), `valid` (the
  expert's verdict), `reason` (why it was rejected, `null` when valid), `note` (the
  expert's remark, `null` when there is none). The reasons:
  - `not-standalone` (5): the question can't be understood without its passage: it
    points to the article, or names nothing (person, place, event) that identifies
    what it asks about;
  - `opinion` (4): it asks what a writer thinks, not a fact;
  - `fiction` (2): a serial novel or a literary text;
  - `not-usable` (2): the passage holds no fact to ask about (stock quotations, an
    advertisement);
  - `vague` (1): several answers would be right, other articles answer otherwise;
  - `ocr-noise` (1): the question rests on a word the OCR misread;
  - `false-premise` (1): the question states something the corpus contradicts;
  - `wrong-answer` (1): the generated answer is wrong.

  No question confused the issue's date with the event's: each date was checked
  against the passage's "hier" or "aujourd'hui". 27 questions name the newspaper and
  its date ("d'après L'Humanité du 5 janvier 1914"), a phrasing readers seldom use and
  that the passages' text doesn't contain; they were kept when otherwise valid (21).
- `annotated-answers.jsonl`: 57 real answers of the labs' RAG, one per line, annotated
  (by an LLM standing in for the annotator, see below): `id`, `question_id` (an `id` of the two files above), `question`,
  `reference_answer` (`null` when the question has none), `configuration` (the lab's
  name of the retrieval that served the passages: `dense`, the dense retrieval alone,
  or `hybride+reclassement`, BM25 and dense fused by RRF, then the first 30 reranked
  by a cross-encoder), `served_passage_ids` (the 5 passages of the prompt, in order),
  `found` (`false` when the system declined), `response` (its answer as it wrote it,
  even when it declined), `cited_passage_ids` (the served passages it cites),
  `faithful` (it states nothing the served passages don't; a refusal is faithful),
  `correct` (it says what the reference answer says; a refusal is correct when the
  question has no answer), `note`. Among them: 28 faithful and correct answers, 11
  unfaithful and wrong, 2 unfaithful but correct, 1 faithful but wrong, 13 wrong
  refusals (12 of them justified by passages that lack the answer: retrieval
  failures) and 2 right ones.
- `training-questions.jsonl`: 619 generated questions for training, not reviewed, one
  per line: `id` (1001 to 1619), `question`, `answer` (the generated short answer),
  `quote` (the excerpt of the passage, copied verbatim with its OCR errors, that bears
  the answer: the citation of a RAFT target answer), `passage_id` (the passage the
  question was generated from, the one known to state the answer), `split` (`train`,
  557, or `validation`, 62). None comes from a passage of the test questions above.

## How they were made

`questions.jsonl` was written by an LLM (Claude) from passages read in full, each
answer checked against its passage, for shuuchuu's text mining labs. The other passages
that state the answers of questions 3 and 8, found by reviewing the retrieval failures,
were added since.

`synthetic-questions.jsonl`: generated by Claude (Sonnet, Anthropic) with the prompt of
the RAG evaluation lab (`genai/rag/evaluation/press-rag-evaluation-ragas` in
shuuchuu/slides, exercise 1), one call per passage, on the lab's sample of 60 passages
(noise at most 0.05, 40 from *L'Humanité* and 20 from *Le Figaro*, `random_state`
1914). The validation, the rewordings and the search for the other passages that state
each answer (BM25 and dense retrieval with the question, the answer and its key terms,
then reading) were done by an LLM, Claude, standing in for the domain expert, in a
context separate from the generation, from each source passage read in full.

`annotated-answers.jsonl`: the answers were generated by Qwen3-1.7B (no thinking,
greedy decoding) with the lab's answer prompt, on 29 questions of the two files above,
each retrieved both ways (`dense`, then `hybride+reclassement`); one answer whose JSON
didn't parse was left out. The annotations were made by an LLM, Claude, standing in
for the human annotator, from the question, the reference answer and the served
passages read in full.

`training-questions.jsonl`: generated by Claude (Sonnet, Anthropic) with the prompt of
`synthetic-questions.jsonl`, plus two instructions (copy a verbatim quote of one to
three sentences, at most 40 words, that bears the answer; refer "hier" and the like to
the issue's date, and decline serial novels, literary texts and opinions), each
passage taken on its own, in batches of 60 to 85 passages per session. The 1,080
passages: noise at most 0.05, two thirds from *L'Humanité*, one third from *Le Figaro*
(`random_state` 2014 for the first 600, 2015 for 480 more), none among the 97 passages
the test questions name (`passage_ids`, `source_passage_id`, rejected questions
included) nor with a cosine similarity above 0.75 to one of them
(`embeddings-512.npy`). The generator found 660 usable; the automatic filter of the RAG
evaluation lab (exercise 2) then dropped 4 that point to the text and 36 whose answer
shares fewer than half its words of four letters or more with the passage (mostly right
answers that OCR noise or a number in figures hides from the check), and every quote was
checked to be in its passage. Of the 25 questions closest to a test question (shared
words), one, on the same event and with the same answer as question 125, was dropped.
The validation split is a random tenth (`numpy` `default_rng(1914)`). No expert read
them: some answers are wrong, as with any generated set.
